### 1. Summarize the six questions asked by Narada Muni to lord Brahma. (2.5.1–2)

**Answer:** Nārada respectfully approaches Brahmā as his spiritual master and asks about six fundamental matters:

1. **Knowledge:** What is transcendental knowledge concerning the soul and the Supersoul?
2. **Form:** What are the characteristics or nature of the manifested world?
3. **Basis:** On what does the cosmic manifestation rest?
4. **Creation:** By what process is the world brought into existence?
5. **Maintenance:** How is the world sustained?
6. **Control:** Under whose ultimate direction does it function?

**Central lesson:** Spiritual inquiry begins with humility and seeks the ultimate cause rather than stopping at visible material mechanisms. Brahmā's answers lead Nārada to recognize Lord Nārāyaṇa as the supreme source.

**References:** [SB 2.5.1](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/1/) · [SB 2.5.2](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/2/)

### 2. What doubts arose in Nārada Muni regarding the position of Brahmājī? (2.5.7)

**Answer:** Nārada knew that Brahmā was the first created being and possessed extraordinary knowledge and the power to organize universal creation. Nevertheless, Brahmā had performed austerities. This gave rise to a genuine doubt: **If Brahmā is the independent supreme controller, why would he need to undertake austerity or receive power from another?**

Nārada therefore considered whether:
- Brahmā was the **ultimate creator** or an **empowered secondary creator**;
- the power of creation was **intrinsic and independent** or **received from a higher source**;
- someone **greater than Brahmā** existed.

Brahmā subsequently dispels the doubt by acknowledging that Lord Nārāyaṇa is the Supreme Lord and that Brahmā's own creative ability depends on Him.

**Central lesson:** Greatness, authority, or extraordinary ability does not necessarily imply absolute supremacy. The empowered servant remains dependent on the Supreme.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.7](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/7/)
