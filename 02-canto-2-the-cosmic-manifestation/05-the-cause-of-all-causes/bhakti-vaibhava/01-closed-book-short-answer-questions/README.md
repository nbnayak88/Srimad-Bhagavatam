# Chapter 5 — Closed-Book Short Answer Questions

**Course:** Bhakti Vaibhava · **Canto 2:** The Cosmic Manifestation · **Chapter:** The Cause of All Causes

**Primary source:** Bhakti Vaibhava Students’ Handbook, Unit 8, p. 2–3.  
**Scriptural reference:** [Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 2.5 — Vedabase](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/).

> Study answers are concise paraphrases for closed-book revision, not quotations from the purports. The handbook's question sequence is preserved.

### 1. What is the regulation of the disciplic succession? (2.5.1)
**Answer:** Transcendental knowledge is received from a bona fide spiritual master by submissive inquiry and service. The disciple hears faithfully from an authorized teacher and then passes on the knowledge without changing its essential meaning. Brahmā taught Nārada in this way.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.1](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/1/)

### 2. List the three things observed by the intelligent man in the phenomenal world as mentioned in the purport of 2.5.2.
**Answer:** The living entities, the manifested material world, and the ultimate controller of both. Intelligent inquiry seeks to understand how these are related.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.2](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/2/)

### 3. Explain the analogy of the walnut that Narada Muni. (2.5.3)
**Answer:** Just as one can hold a walnut in one's palm and inspect it, Nārada describes Brahmā as knowing the universe's affairs—its past, present, and future—as though they were within his grasp. The comparison emphasizes Brahmā's extraordinary knowledge, not his absolute independence.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.3](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/3/)

### 4. Why could Narada Muni understand that there was someone else superior to Brahmājī who invested Brahmā with the power of creation? (2.5.4)
**Answer:** Nārada saw that Brahmā had performed severe austerities to obtain creative power. Such austerity suggested that Brahmā depended on a superior source who could empower him.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.4](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/4/)

### 5. What is the best example of self-sufficiency? (2.5.5)
**Answer:** The sun, which illuminates other things by its own light rather than needing another lamp to illuminate it. This helps illustrate the meaning of self-sufficiency.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.5](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/5/)

### 6. Explain the analogy of the spider given in 2.5.5.
**Answer:** A spider produces its web from its own bodily substance. Nārada uses this image to express why Brahmā appeared to be creating the universe from his own potency. The subsequent discussion clarifies that Brahmā's power is actually bestowed by the Supreme Lord.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.5](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/5/)

### 7. Give the meaning of the word ahangrahopasita. (2.5.7)
**Answer:** *Ahaṅgrahopāsitā* refers to worship centered on the conception that the individual self is the Supreme, or the attempt to identify oneself with God. The individual soul is not independently the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.7](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/7/)

### 8. What is the basic principle of missionary activities? (2.5.9)
**Answer:** A devotee who receives genuine transcendental knowledge wishes to share it for others' spiritual welfare. Hearing and speaking about the Supreme Lord benefit both the speaker and the listener.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.9](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/9/)

### 9. What is the meaning of devā nārāyaṇāṅgajāḥ? (2.5.15)
**Answer:** The demigods arise from, or are related as parts to, Lord Nārāyaṇa; they depend on Him and carry out their respective functions under His supreme authority.

**Reference:** [SB 2.5.15](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/5/15/)
